# Research: a real photo or video of a person to a 3D model for clothes

Searched on GitHub and the web in late November 2026; every repository below was then checked independently by reading its README, LICENSE and source in a shallow clone. Hugging Face, arXiv and some project pages were not reachable from the research machine, so licence tags of model weights on Hugging Face are second-hand: re-check them before any commercial use.

## Bottom line

- There is no single tool that turns one photo, or a phone video of a person turning round, into a clean, textured, commercially usable 3D person with correct clothes. It has to be assembled.
- Human-specific research code (the implicit-function line, multi-view diffusion humans, large human reconstruction models) is either licensed for research only (body-model and dataset licences, non-commercial weights, or no licence file at all) or needs 24 to 48 GB of GPU memory, and several repositories do not export a mesh at all.
- From a single photo the back is always invented. The back print of this shirt would be hallucinated; it has to come from the brand's own artwork as a texture.
- A turning person filmed with a fixed phone is the hard case for photogrammetry and splatting: the body is not rigid, feet step, light slides over the cloth. Expect a usable torso at best.

## What to use for this project

1. **Figure:** `makehumancommunity/mpfb2` inside Blender: a parametric human whose assets and output are CC0, no GPU needed, scriptable from Python. Match proportions and pose by eye to the video. Optional helper: `facebookresearch/sam-3d-body` estimates a body shape and pose from a photo (commercial use allowed with conditions; under loose clothes the shape is a guess).
2. **Clothes:** model the shirt from its real measurements and drape it with cloth simulation in Blender. `maria-korosteleva/GarmentCode` gives programmable sewing patterns under MIT (use its pattern output; its own draping step depends on a non-commercial simulator fork). `st-tech/ppf-contact-solver` is an Apache-2.0 contact solver with a Blender 5 add-on for higher quality draping; its own FAQ calls it not production ready, so treat it as an option, with Blender's built-in cloth as the default.
3. **Prints:** the brand's own artwork as textures on decals. Never from a generator.
4. **Optional experiment, as a shape reference only:** a general image-to-3D generator (`microsoft/TRELLIS.2` through `Comfy-Org/ComfyUI`, or the desktop app `lightningpixel/modly`) on a cut-out photo. Needs an NVIDIA card (about 8 GB for the small models, 16 to 24 GB for TRELLIS.2). Mind the licences: the official TRELLIS.2 GLB export path uses a non-commercial rasteriser; ComfyUI's own implementation is reported to avoid it. A real person's likeness needs that person's consent.

## Verified repositories

### Comfy-Org/ComfyUI

- Verdict: **recommend**; commercial use: yes-with-conditions; effort for a non-expert: medium; CPU realistic: no; stars 148693; last push 2026-11-26
- Code licence: GPL-3.0 (LICENSE file is the GNU GPL v3 text). It covers the program, not the meshes it produces, and matters only if the brand redistributes ComfyUI itself.
- Weights licence: ComfyUI ships no weights; the licence depends on the model loaded. TRELLIS.2: MIT for 'model and code' (microsoft/TRELLIS.2 README line 314). Pixal3D: MIT for 'code, parameters, weights' (TencentARC/Pixal3D NOTICE). SAM 3D Body: SAM License. Hunyuan3D 2.1: not checked here. The Comfy-Org repackaged weights on Hugging Face could not be opened.
- Non-commercial dependencies: None found in ComfyUI core for the TRELLIS.2 and Pixal3D path. A Comfy blog snippet says the native implementation replaces the non-commercial nvdiffrast and nvdiffrec dependencies of the original pipeline; the core node file imports only torch and ComfyUI modules. Third-party custom node packs may still pull in nvdiffrast or non-commercial background-removal models, so stick to the core nodes. Partner/API nodes are paid closed services with their own terms.
- Input: One photo; the template removes the background and estimates camera FOV. Core also has a 'Pixal3D Multi-View Conditioning' node and Hunyuan3D multi-view conditioning, so several frames from the turning video could be fed in (not tested).
- Output: A textured GLB by default, saved under output/3d/. The 'Save 3D Model' node also accepts GLTF, OBJ, FBX, STL, USDZ and PLY files. Core mesh nodes include Decimate Mesh (target face count, default 200,000), Unwrap Mesh UVs, Bake Texture From Voxel/Views, Bake Normal Map, Fill Holes, Weld Vertices and Remesh. Core SAM3D Body nodes produce an animated body GLB or BVH.
- Hardware: ComfyUI itself runs on NVIDIA, AMD (ROCm), Intel Arc, Apple Silicon or CPU-only (docs/installation.md); the README claims large models run on 'as low as 4GB vram + 8GB ram'. For TRELLIS.2, Microsoft's README asks for an NVIDIA GPU with at least 24 GB for the original pipeline. Third-party guides claim about 8 GB in low-VRAM mode at 512 and 16 GB or more for 1024; these are unverified. I found no confirmation that the native 3D nodes work on AMD or Mac.
- Hosted demo or notebook: Comfy Cloud (comfy.org/cloud) offers the 'Pixal3D & TRELLIS.2: Image to Model' template. Search snippets say 6 credits per run and '5 free runs, no credit card' (older posts say 400 free credits a month); paid plans start around $16-20 a month. Pricing is from snippets only. Comfy Desktop is a normal installer for Windows and macOS.
- Quality notes: ComfyUI is a runner, so quality is that of TRELLIS.2 or Pixal3D. Both are general object models, not human-specific: from one photo the back of the shirt is invented, and faces and hands of a full-body figure are usually weak. I could not confirm the searcher's quote that closed generators still beat them (blog.comfy.org is blocked and search did not surface it). TRELLIS.2 is described as a 4B-parameter model with PBR materials and up to 1536 effective resolution.
- Corrections found during verification: Pixal3D weights are MIT per its NOTICE; the claim of a custom EU-excluding licence is wrong for the repo as cloned (Hunyuan3D not checked). ComfyUI is not NVIDIA-only: its docs cover AMD, Intel, Apple Silicon and CPU mode. It does have a hosted option, Comfy Cloud, with this exact template. Core also ships SAM 3D Body nodes and a full mesh clean-up toolkit, which the searcher did not mention. The 8 GB minimum rests on third-party guides only. Native TRELLIS.2 and Pixal3D nodes, GPL-3.0, stars and push date are confirmed.

### microsoft/TRELLIS.2

- Verdict: **recommend**; commercial use: yes-with-conditions; effort for a non-expert: medium; CPU realistic: no; stars 13239; last push 2026-07-10
- Code licence: MIT (LICENSE file read, 'Copyright (c) Microsoft Corporation'). README: 'This model and code are released under the MIT License', with nvdiffrast and nvdiffrec named as separately licensed dependencies.
- Weights licence: MIT per the README sentence above, and the Hugging Face card microsoft/TRELLIS.2-4B is reported as 'license: mit' by a search summary. I could not open huggingface.co from this session.
- Non-commercial dependencies: Four things to know. (1) nvdiffrast: NVIDIA Source Code License (1-Way Commercial), non-commercial only (raw LICENSE.txt read for main and tag v0.4.0). The official GLB export o_voxel.postprocess.to_glb imports it (line 10) and uses it to bake the texture (lines 230-249). (2) nvdiffrec fork (branch renderutils): non-commercial NVIDIA licence, used for the preview video only. (3) Background remover: the official pipeline config is reported to load briaai/RMBG-2.0, which is CC BY-NC 4.0 (second-hand: search summary and issue #22). The code skips background removal when the input PNG already has transparency, but the model is still loaded at start. (4) DINOv3 image encoder (gated on Hugging Face, Meta 'DINOv3 License'): mandatory; reported to permit commercial use with acceptable-use and trade-control terms.
- Input: One image of the subject. The official app takes a single RGBA image; a PNG with a transparent background is used as-is. No multi-image input in the official pipeline. A second pipeline textures an existing mesh from an image.
- Output: GLB with PBR textures (base colour, roughness, metallic, opacity) through o_voxel.postprocess.to_glb. The demo exposes a decimation target from 100,000 to 1,000,000 (default 500,000) and texture size 1024 to 4096, so about 100k to 150k is a setting, not extra work. Exported opaque by default. Also an MP4 preview.
- Hardware: Official README: Linux only, 'An NVIDIA GPU with at least 24GB of memory is necessary', verified on A100 and H100; CUDA Toolkit 12.4 needed to compile several extensions. Community routes seen in search results but not tested: ComfyUI core support since v0.34.0 (release notes: 'feat: Support Pixal3d and TRELLIS2'); a ComfyUI wrapper with prebuilt Windows wheels (visualbruno/ComfyUI-Trellis2, MIT, 894 stars); quantised GGUF builds reported to fit 8 GB at 512 resolution and 16 GB or more for 1024 (third-party figures); MLX ports for Apple Silicon listed on Hugging Face.
- Hosted demo or notebook: Official Hugging Face Space microsoft/TRELLIS.2 is linked from the README; whether it is up today was not checked (huggingface.co unreachable here). No official Colab. Two users asked in issue #22 (November 2026) whether assets from the Space may be used commercially; no answer yet.
- Quality notes: README claims open surfaces such as clothing are handled, and 1024 resolution takes about 17 s on an H100. The model card is reported to say raw meshes can have small holes and that the model is not tuned to human preferences. It is a general object model: I found no independent test on photos of real people; a Hugging Face discussion asking about human bodies has no answer, and issue #46 reports distorted results. From one photo the back of the person and of the T-shirt is invented, so a back print will be wrong unless re-textured from the other photos. Expect faces and hands to look off.
- Corrections found during verification: (1) 'Plain MIT, clean anywhere' is too strong: the official GLB export needs nvdiffrast, which is non-commercial, and the default background remover is reported as CC BY-NC. The maintainer wrote on 2025-12-24 that the NVIDIA libraries are 'used only for visualization', but the code shows to_glb uses nvdiffrast. (2) A commercially clean route is reported: ComfyUI's built-in implementation is said to replace the NVIDIA parts with its own PyTorch code (Comfy blog, seen only through a search summary). (3) The official Space is microsoft/TRELLIS.2, linked in the README. (4) to_glb's docstring calls decimation_target a vertex target while app.py calls it a face target, so check the final triangle count. (5) Stars, licence tag and the 24 GB figure are confirmed.

### facebookresearch/sam2

- Verdict: **usable**; commercial use: yes; effort for a non-expert: medium; CPU realistic: yes; stars 20243; last push 2026-05-30
- Code licence: Apache-2.0 (LICENSE read: Apache License Version 2.0, 201 lines; GitHub API agrees). Third-party connected-components code under BSD 3-Clause (LICENSE_cctorch).
- Weights licence: Apache-2.0. README License section: 'The SAM 2 model checkpoints, SAM 2 demo code (front-end and back-end), and SAM 2 training code are licensed under Apache 2.0'. Demo fonts are SIL OFL 1.1.
- Non-commercial dependencies: none found (core requirements in setup.py: torch>=2.5.1, torchvision, numpy, tqdm, hydra-core, iopath, pillow)
- Input: A video (or folder of frames) or a single image, plus a prompt: clicks or a box on the person in one frame.
- Output: Per-frame binary masks for the prompted object, returned to Python (propagate_in_video yields frame_idx, object_ids, masks). Saving them as PNG files for COLMAP or a splat trainer needs a few lines of script; hard-edged masks, no soft alpha matte. Not a 3D tool.
- Hardware: Built for an NVIDIA GPU: README says 'install SAM 2 on a GPU machine', speeds 91 FPS (tiny) to 39.5 FPS (large) on an A100; VRAM not stated. The CUDA extension is optional (README note 3: build failure can be ignored). CPU and Apple MPS are supported but slow: demo/README.md line 3 says the backend runs 'locally on MPS (Metal Performance Shaders) or CPU' with 'significantly slower performance', and the video notebook selects cuda / mps / cpu and warns MPS support is preliminary. One third-party model card reports about 2 s per frame (6 threads) to 5 s per frame (2 threads) for the tiny model on a laptop i7-13620H; a single source. Windows: WSL 'strongly recommended'.
- Hosted demo or notebook: Yes. Hosted demo https://sam2.metademolab.com/ (whether it lets you download raw masks was not checked); official Colab notebooks linked from the README for image prediction, automatic masks and video prediction; a self-hostable web demo (Docker).
- Quality notes: The README gives only benchmark scores (SA-V, MOSE, LVOS) and no statement about people or clothing. Independent reports found concern memory, not mask quality: GPU memory grows with video length until out-of-memory, eased by the predictor's offload_video_to_cpu / offload_state_to_cpu options. A 9-second clip is short enough that this should not bite. Masks are binary, so hair and loose fabric edges are cut hard; fine as COLMAP feature masks, rougher as a final cut-out.
- Corrections found during verification: (1) 'CPU use not documented' is wrong: demo/README.md and the notebooks document CPU and MPS use, with a speed warning. (2) 'Install compiles a CUDA kernel' is optional; the README says a failed build can be ignored. (3) The 2026-05-30 push date does not reflect new code on the default branch (last commit December 2024). (4) Official Colab notebooks exist and were not mentioned. Licence and star claims confirmed. The searcher's side claims about sam3, BiRefNet, rembg, RobustVideoMatting and MatAnyone were not verified here.

### colmap/colmap

- Verdict: **usable**; commercial use: yes-with-conditions; effort for a non-expert: high; CPU realistic: yes; stars 13299; last push 2026-11-26
- Code licence: BSD 3-clause ('new BSD'), read in LICENSE and README; GitHub API shows NOASSERTION. The licence text warns: 'Building COLMAP with these dependencies may affect the resulting COLMAP license.'
- Weights licence: None for the default SIFT pipeline. The optional learned features (ALIKED, LoMa, need an ONNX-enabled build) have model files whose licences I did not check.
- Non-commercial dependencies: Yes, one to avoid: the bundled GPU SIFT code, src/thirdparty/SiftGPU/LICENSE, grants use only 'for educational, research and non-profit purposes'. Running feature extraction and matching on CPU (use_gpu=false) avoids that code path. Also bundled or linked copyleft parts: src/thirdparty/LSD is AGPL, and CGAL (GPL / LGPL per search results) is a build dependency; copyleft matters if you redistribute COLMAP binaries, not for using its output.
- Input: A set of photos or frames extracted from the video. Optional per-image masks: doc/faq.rst lines 459-473, mask_path folder with files named like image.jpg.png, no features where the mask is black.
- Output: Camera poses and a sparse point cloud in COLMAP format. With an NVIDIA CUDA GPU also dense depth maps, a fused point cloud, Poisson or Delaunay mesh (PLY), a decimated mesh (mesh_simplifier) and, since 4.0.0, a textured mesh from mesh_texturer, written as mesh.ply plus texture.png (src/colmap/exe/mvs.cc lines 179-194). No GLB or OBJ directly; convert in MeshLab or Blender, and whether Blender reads the UVs from that PLY was not tested.
- Hardware: Sparse reconstruction (poses) runs on CPU: doc/faq.rst lines 541-557, set --FeatureExtraction.use_gpu=false, expect a 'significant slow-down' and high RAM on large images. Dense stereo (patch_match_stereo) needs an NVIDIA CUDA GPU: 'you can use all COLMAP functionality except the dense reconstruction part' without one. VRAM not stated. Mac build exists (tutorial mentions COLMAP.app) but without CUDA, so no dense step there.
- Hosted demo or notebook: No hosted demo or official notebook. Desktop GUI with 'automatic reconstruction' in one click, Windows binaries, conda, Docker.
- Quality notes: COLMAP assumes a rigid scene. Its FAQ and tutorial contain no guidance for a moving subject (grep for moving / turntable / rotating found nothing relevant); the tutorial asks for 'good texture' and warns against texture-less images, which is a problem for plain fabric. Forum threads found by search concern a different photogrammetry program but describe the same setup: the static background must be masked or it drives alignment, a person's breathing and small shifts spoil alignment and cause mesh errors, continuous rotation gives motion blur, and the subject should fill the frame. Advantage over single-photo methods: the real back of the shirt is observed, not invented. Hands and face are the parts most likely to smear because they move. CPU-only users get poses and a sparse cloud, not a mesh.
- Corrections found during verification: (1) 'COLMAP's own meshes are untextured' is outdated: COLMAP 4.0.0 (03/14/2026) added mesh texture mapping and QEM mesh simplification (CHANGELOG.rst lines 397-398; doc/cli.rst lines 95-104, 435; doc/faq.rst lines 753-757). openMVS or mvs-texturing are no longer required for a textured mesh, though the dense step still needs CUDA. (2) Licence is not unconditionally clean: bundled SiftGPU is restricted to educational, research and non-profit use, so a business should run SIFT on CPU. (3) The current flag is --FeatureExtraction.use_gpu=false. Mask naming, CPU SfM, CUDA-only dense stereo, absence of moving-subject guidance, stars and push date confirmed. openMVS and mvs-texturing claims not verified.

### nerfstudio-project/nerfstudio

- Verdict: **usable**; commercial use: yes; effort for a non-expert: high; CPU realistic: no; stars 12263; last push 2025-07-29
- Code licence: Apache-2.0 (LICENSE file read; pyproject.toml also says 'Apache 2.0').
- Weights licence: Not applicable: it optimises a model per capture, with no pretrained weights and no body model.
- Non-commercial dependencies: None found in the default route (COLMAP, FFmpeg, nerfacc, gsplat 1.4.0, xatlas, pymeshlab, open3d as listed in pyproject.toml; I did not read each dependency's licence here). Caution, not verified in this session: the optional hloc feature matchers have their own licences, so stay with the default COLMAP matching.
- Input: A video or a photo set. ns-process-data extracts frames and runs COLMAP for camera poses. Optional per-frame masks via mask_path in transforms.json: one channel, same size as the image, black means ignore, and if used every image must have one.
- Output: ns-export tsdf or ns-export poisson writes a textured mesh as .obj with a texture image (xatlas unwrap, default 2048 px per side, default target_num_faces 50,000, which is inside the 150k budget). Also point cloud .ply and gaussian-splat .ply. No GLB: import the OBJ in Blender and export GLB.
- Hardware: README: 'You must have an NVIDIA video card with CUDA installed', tested with CUDA 11.8. VRAM is not stated. Linux recommended; the docs call Windows 'less tested and more fragile'. A Docker image exists. No Mac path.
- Hosted demo or notebook: colab/demo.ipynb is linked from the README and has an 'upload your own video' option. It pins torch 2.0.1+cu118 and a prebuilt Python 3.10 tiny-cuda-nn wheel, so it may no longer run unchanged on current Colab (not tested). No hosted demo.
- Quality notes: This reconstructs what was really filmed, so the real shirt and real back print, but only for a static subject. The docs describe masks as a way to remove 'moving objects such as people', and warn that masking 'is inefficient and will cause large memory allocations'. The brand's clip is the opposite case: a person turning on the spot. That needs the background masked out in every frame so that COLMAP registers on the person alone, and independent sources report COLMAP failing on most dynamic clips even with correct motion masks. Nine seconds gives few sharp frames, a plain T-shirt has little texture to match, and any arm, head or cloth movement during the turn smears. It becomes a good option if they re-shoot: person standing still in an A-pose while the camera walks a full circle for 30 to 60 seconds. Raw meshes need cleanup in Blender. Faces and hands come out soft.
- Corrections found during verification: Claims mostly confirmed (licence, OBJ export, mask rules, Colab link, stars, dates). Refinements: (1) the export doc says TSDF 'works for all models', but nerfstudio's own splatfacto page is reported as saying mesh export from splats is not supported, so use nerfacto with predict-normals plus Poisson, not splatfacto, when a mesh is the goal. (2) Default export is already 50,000 faces. (3) The Colab notebook is old and may be broken. (4) The existing 9-second turning clip is a poor fit; treat this as a route for a re-shoot.

### st-tech/ppf-contact-solver

- Verdict: **usable**; commercial use: yes; effort for a non-expert: high; CPU realistic: yes; stars 4621; last push 2026-11-26
- Code licence: Apache-2.0 for the solver and Python frontend (LICENSE). GPL-3.0-or-later for blender_addon/, examples/blender/ and addon_host_tests/ (LICENSING.md). The GPL part matters only if you redistribute add-on code, not for meshes you simulate.
- Weights licence: Not applicable: a physics solver, no ML weights and no body model.
- Non-commercial dependencies: none found. README: "Apache 2.0 allows commercial and proprietary use"; articles/commercial_use.md repeats it.
- Input: A garment mesh (shell), a body or collider mesh, pins, stitches and fabric parameters, set up in Blender 5.0+ through the add-on, its Python API, its bundled MCP server, or in JupyterLab notebooks. It takes no photo or video: you must already have the garment and body meshes.
- Output: Simulated cloth deformation fetched back into Blender. "Bake Animation" writes shape keys; "Export USD" and "Export Alembic" write point caches; notebooks export PLY sequences. GLB or OBJ comes from Blender's own exporter afterwards. It creates no textures.
- Hardware: README requirements table: NVIDIA Turing or newer (RTX 20 / GTX 16 / T4) with driver 580+; RTX 3090/4070/5070 for small to medium scenes. AMD through ROCm, Apple silicon (M1+) through Metal, and a CPU backend on any x86_64/arm64 machine. README: "CPU and Metal backends are slow: Meant for evaluation, learning, and small examples." Blender can stay on a laptop while the solver runs on a rented GPU (README: vast.ai "for less than $0.5 per hour"; guides for vast.ai, Scaleway, AWS, GCE). Needs Blender 5.0 or newer.
- Hosted demo or notebook: No hosted demo and no Colab. Local JupyterLab at localhost:8080 ships with the solver; a vast.ai template link and a Docker image are provided. The add-on installs as a Blender extension from a repository URL.
- Quality notes: Does not reconstruct anything, so back views and textures are not its concern. What it gives is physically draped cloth: no intersections when a step succeeds, a strain limit so cloth is "not rubbery", and six fabric presets (Cotton, Denim, Silk, Wool and others) calibrated against measurements. The repo's own FAQ: "Should we use it? Honestly, not yet for serious use" and "Not production ready (yet)". Its criticism page (video comments collected 2026-11-12) lists: slow, not real time, slower than Marvelous Designer, runs that do not complete, cloth clipping into a static collider, hard install, hard-to-find parameters, poor behaviour with animated colliders (partly fixed). I found no timing for a single T-shirt on CPU or Mac, so "CPU is realistic" rests only on the README's "small examples" wording. A marching-cubes mesh at 256 with vertex colours would give soft textures and blurred shirt prints. The SMPL prior is coarse, so loose clothing is a plausible weak point.
- Corrections found during verification: The released script does not save meshes at all, so 'GLB plausible' is wrong; it writes renders and a video only. Confirmed: no licence, 61 stars, last push 2026-06-01, SMPLify and HMR2 dependency.

## State of the field, by angle

### single-photo-human

The classic implicit-function line (PIFu/PIFuHD, ICON, ECON, SIFU, SiTH, TeCH) is effectively frozen: last pushes are 2023 to 2025, PIFuHD was archived in Aug 2025, and almost all of it is tied to the SMPL/SMPL-X body model and Max Planck or CC-BY-NC non-commercial terms; it gives usable geometry but weak or invented back texture. The 2024-2025 step was multi-view diffusion plus explicit mesh carving (PSHuman, Human3Diffusion, MagicMan), which finally produced photoreal textured meshes with a plausible back, at the price of heavy hardware (PSHuman needs over 40 GB VRAM). In parallel, feed-forward large human reconstruction models (LHM, LHM++ open-sourced March 2026, IDOL, HumanNOVA released May 2026) brought the time down to about a second on 8 to 24 GB cards and made avatars animatable, but they output 3D Gaussians bound to an SMPL-X skeleton rather than a clean mesh, the LHM maintainers explicitly refuse mesh export, and the LHM/LHM++ weights are CC BY-NC. The newest direction synchronises 2D multi-view diffusion with a native 3D generator (SyncHuman on TRELLIS), drops the body prior, and writes a GLB directly; it is the closest match to this brand's goal but is a five-commit repo with no licence file, no demo and no stated VRAM. Several headline 2026 papers have no confirmed code (HumanOrbit, MultiGO++, OAHuman), Pippo is code-only without weights, AniGS was never released, HumanSplat and IDOL weights could not be confirmed, CharacterGen (Apache-2.0) targets anime characters, and TeCH needs about 3 hours on 2x32 GB GPUs. Still weak everywhere: the unseen back is hallucinated (a back print on the T-shirt will be invented from one photo), hands, hair and faces, very loose garments, and the garment is fused with the body into one surface with dense messy topology and usually vertex colours, so Blender decimation and a texture bake are always needed before three.js. Licence-wise no human-specific single-photo option found is cleanly commercial (SMPL-X licences, non-commercial scan datasets, NC weights or missing licence files). Two adjacent routes surfaced in the same searches and deserve the other searchers' attention: general image-to-3D generators that export GLB under permissive code licences (microsoft/TRELLIS 14.4k stars and microsoft/TRELLIS.2 13.2k stars, both MIT; TencentARC/Pixal3D 2.7k stars, MIT; Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2 and 2.1 under a custom licence), and methods that use several photos or the 9-second turn-around video (LHM++ multi-view mode, zcai0612/UP2You ICLR 2026) so that the back is observed instead of guessed; facebookresearch/sam-3d-body (3.8k stars, SAM License) gives only an unclothed parametric body. Access note: huggingface.co, arxiv.org and github.io project pages were not reachable from this session, so weight-licence tags on Hugging Face cards and paper limitation sections come from search snippets and GitHub READMEs only and should be re-checked before any commercial decision.

### video-and-multiview

There is still no maintained, permissively licensed, one-command tool that takes a video of a turning person and returns a clean textured GLB; the result has to be assembled from parts. Route one treats the clip as a turntable scan: mask the person in every frame (SAM 2), solve poses on the masked frames (COLMAP, or feed-forward VGGT / MapAnything / Depth Anything 3 when COLMAP fails), train a splat or NeRF, then extract and decimate a mesh. That only works as far as the body is rigid: the torso and T-shirt usually survive, while feet that step to turn, arms and head smear, and room light sliding over the rotating body bakes into blotchy texture; forum practitioners report centimetre-scale balance movements and some advise against human turntables altogether. Route two is the human-specific monocular avatar family (Vid2Avatar, SelfRecon, InstantAvatar, GaussianAvatar, ExAvatar, DressRecon), which is benchmarked on exactly this capture and models the motion, but it is research code needing hours to days on a large NVIDIA GPU, mostly outputs gaussians or smooth meshes without UV textures, and nearly all of it depends on SMPL / SMPL-X, which is non-commercial unless licensed through Meshcapade. What changed in 2025-2026: feed-forward pose and geometry models (VGGT with a gated commercial checkpoint, MapAnything, Depth Anything 3) made pose estimation far less fragile; LHM++ produces an animatable gaussian avatar from a few unposed frames in about a second on 8 GB; SAM 3 / 3.1 added text-prompted video masks; MILo added mesh-in-the-loop splatting; Brush and Spark brought splat training and three.js display to almost any GPU and the browser; and image-to-3D generators with multi-image input (TRELLIS, Hunyuan3D-2mv) now give a closed textured GLB straight from three or four frames. Licences are the weak point for a brand that sells: the best splat-to-mesh extractors (2DGS, SuGaR, MILo) carry Inria's non-commercial licence, LHM++ weights are CC BY-NC, Hunyuan3D's licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea, and TRELLIS is MIT but exports through nvdiffrast under an Nvidia licence I did not read. Hardware is the other: almost everything needs an NVIDIA CUDA card; without one, only COLMAP's pose step, Meshroom's draft meshing, OpenSplat on CPU (about 100x slower) and Brush on other GPUs are realistic, plus hosted demos (TRELLIS, LHM++, SAM 2) and free Colab T4 sessions, which a 2026 write-up found adequate for short splat trainings. For this brand the 9-second clip is best used as a source of a few sharp stills for a multi-image generator, with the classical route as the faithful but fiddly alternative; if they can re-shoot, walking the phone slowly around a person standing frozen makes the classical route much more reliable than having the person turn. Limits of this search: GitHub keyword searches for recent work returned almost nothing, so recency rests on web search and README reads; www.frontiersin.org and lingtengqiu.github.io could not be reached and would have to be allowed in the session's network settings to read the smartphone-avatar paper and the LHM++ project page.

### garments

In late 2026 the clothes-specific corner has converged on one idea: instead of carving a garment out of a scanned person, a vision-language model reads the photo and writes a parametric sewing pattern (almost always in GarmentCode format), which is then sewn and draped by a cloth simulator; ChatGarment, AIpparel and Design2GarmentCode (all CVPR 2025) have released code, and OmniFabric (SIGGRAPH Asia 2026) is the first released pipeline I found that adds a clean UV texture and writes a textured garment GLB from one photo. The newest single-image papers listed in the fashion paper lists (Dress-1-to-3, DressWild, ReWeaver, NGL-Prompter, GarmentGPT, SwiftTailor, GarmentHiFi) had no code link that I could confirm, so the usable state of the art is still the 2025 models. What is weak: results are plausible garments of the right type rather than exact copies, sizes need manual correction, textures and prints are a separate and much heavier step (NI-Tex and OmniFabric want 48 GB or H100-class GPUs), and almost nothing runs on CPU or a free notebook. Licensing is the main trap for a brand that sells clothes: the GarmentCode simulator fork is non-commercial by its own README, most checkpoints have no licence statement, Garment3DGen is CC BY-NC 4.0, GarmageNet is CC BY-NC-ND with no weights, OmniFabric pulls in non-commercial FLUX weights and community licences with territorial exclusions (including the EU), and Sewformer and DressCode need Autodesk Maya for draping. The clearly commercial-safe pieces are the non-AI tools: GarmentCode's MIT pattern code, ZOZO's Apache-2.0 contact solver with its Blender 5 add-on (new this year, very active, can use a rented GPU), MPFB2 with CC0 bodies and clothes, Blender's own cloth modifier, and small projects such as ClothCraft; the polished Blender garment add-ons (Simply Cloth Studio 2.0, Garment Tool, Garment Studio) are paid, and a free GPL extension called Easy Cloth was only visible on a marketplace page I could not open, with no repository found. True 3D try-on with geometry is still thin: GS-VTON (yukangcao/GS-VTON, 239 stars, no licence, built on non-commercial IDM-VTON) edits a Gaussian-splat capture rather than producing a mesh, Gaussian Garments (eth-ait, 174 stars) needs multi-view video and manual UV work, NGD (astonishingwolf/NGD, 11 stars) does monocular garment reconstruction but needs SMPL fits and a template mesh, and Cloth2Tex (HumanAIGC, 525 stars, GPL-3.0) released only its coarse first phase; 2D try-on is far more mature and now has an Apache-2.0 option (FASHN VTON 1.5) beside the non-commercial IDM-VTON. For this brand the clothes angle is therefore best used as a second layer: get the person from the photo or video with a whole-body method from the other angles, and if a clean, editable T-shirt is wanted, build it from a GarmentCode or MPFB2 template (optionally initialised by ChatGarment on a rented GPU), drape it with Blender or the ZOZO solver, apply the brand's own print artwork to the UV in Blender and export GLB. Two notes for the next stage: amirmushichge/interactive-garment-starter (217 stars, MIT) shows a no-3D alternative that turns front/back photos into a scrubbable video turn on a product page, which their existing 9-second turnaround video could feed directly; and repositories such as frrichardson/marvelous-designer-12 that appear in GitHub search look like unofficial redistributions of commercial software and should not be downloaded. Limits of this search: project pages on github.io, the Blender marketplace and the ETH data page were not reachable from this session, so the GarmentCodeData licence, the hosted GarmentCode configurator, Easy Cloth and the Dress-1-to-3 code status are unconfirmed, and VRAM figures marked as estimates are mine, not the authors'.

### general-and-body

By late 2026 single-photo-to-textured-mesh is a solved-enough commodity for objects and stylised characters: Microsoft's TRELLIS.2 (MIT code and weights) and Tencent's Hunyuan3D 2.x are the two open reference models, with Pixal3D (pixel-aligned, on the TRELLIS.2 backbone) the notable 2026 addition, and all of them export GLB that Blender and three.js take directly. What changed in the last year is mostly access rather than quality: ComfyUI now runs TRELLIS.2 and Pixal3D natively, quantised GGUF builds and a C++ port (trellis.cpp, CUDA and Vulkan) brought the 24 GB official requirement down to roughly 8 to 16 GB by community reports, and desktop apps such as Modly wrap the models for people who do not use Python. Licences are the real dividing line for a commercial brand: TRELLIS.2, TripoSR and TripoSG are MIT, Stable Fast 3D is free under 1M USD revenue, while the Tencent family is restricted (Hunyuan3D's licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea; Pixal3D's weights have a custom licence and block EU downloads), and default background removers bundled with several repos are non-commercial. The request was written in Lithuanian, so the EU exclusion probably applies and TRELLIS.2 is the safe default. On the body side the field finally has permissive alternatives to the non-commercial SMPL family: Meta's MHR (Apache-2.0) with SAM 3D Body as the photo-to-body estimator (custom SAM licence, commercial use allowed per secondary sources), NAVER's Anny (Apache-2.0 plus CC0), NVIDIA's SOMA-X and GEM-X (Apache-2.0, March 2026), and MPFB2 as the CC0 human generator inside Blender. What is still weak: every general model sees one view, so the back of the shirt and the person is invented, real faces and hands come out uncanny, and none of them separates the garment from the body or gives true fabric measurements; body estimators return a bare body with no clothes or texture; nothing in this angle runs usefully on a CPU except the body models and Blender tools. Comparison articles are mostly vendor-adjacent blogs that disagree with each other, and one Comfy post reportedly concedes that closed services (Hunyuan 3D hosted, Tripo, Rodin) still look better than the open models. Seen in search but not recommended or not verified: img2threejs/img2threejs (21957 stars, generates procedural three.js code, not a photo-real person), Bingeljell/image-to-3dlab (1282 stars, self-described all-in-one pipeline, claims unverified), speridlabs/idol-sdk (1217 stars, single image to Gaussians, unverified), facebookresearch/sam-3d-objects (7759 stars, objects rather than people) and VAST-AI-Research/UniRig (2364 stars, MIT, auto-rigging if the figure must be posed). Hardware numbers, TripoSR-on-CPU and the Hunyuan multi-view variant are the least verified facts here, because direct page fetches were blocked in this session and licence files could not be read first-hand.
